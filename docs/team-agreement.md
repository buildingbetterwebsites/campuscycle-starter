# Team agreement

How your team works together. Every member reads it at the start, and you read it again when something
goes wrong. Fill in every place marked TODO. The boxes marked Tip are guidance: the course's checks do
not read them, and you may delete them when you are done.

## Members, roles and pairs

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** List each member with their role, then the pairs. Use first names or GitHub usernames
> only, because your repository is public. A role means that you make sure the work happens. Every
> member still writes code and documents.
>
> The six roles:
> - Releases and repository
> - Content model and data
> - The informational task
> - The transactional task
> - Design and accessibility
> - Research and testing
>
> The default for each team size:
> - **Six members:** each member holds one role. The team works in three pairs: one pair for each of
>   your two user tasks, and one pair for the content model with releases.
> - **Five members:** one member holds both *releases and repository* and *content model and data*.
>   That member is the third pair.
> - **Four members:** the same, and the member with *design and accessibility* also holds *research
>   and testing*. The team works in two pairs: each task role with one of the two members who hold
>   two roles.
>
> Your team may share the roles differently, for example to suit what each member does best. Then
> write how, and why.
>
> **Example:** Aya (the transactional task) and Ben (design and accessibility) are the pair for task 1,
> reserve a seat at an event.

## How we decide

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** Two or three lines are enough. Write:
> - what a role holder decides alone;
> - what needs the pair, and what needs the whole team;
> - how you settle a disagreement, and when a decision gets a decision record (in `docs/decisions`).
>
> **Example:** Inside a role, the role holder decides and tells the team. A change to the content
> model needs the content-model pair. A change to a user task's steps, or to the brief, needs the whole
> team. If we cannot agree in one meeting, the role holder decides and writes a decision record.

## When a pull request is done

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** A pull request changes the code. A document such as this one needs no pull request:
> you commit it straight to `main`. Write your own list of what must be true before a pull request is
> merged. This list is the course's default:
> - You tried the change on its preview, at a narrow width too when something visible changed.
> - Its AI note is filled in.
> - The starter's checks have finished. You read what they say, and you fixed what a red check found
>   when you could.
> - A change to the content model has its migration. Only the content-model pair creates migrations.
>
> You merge your own pull request. A teammate's review is optional: write when your team asks for one,
> or that it asks for none.
>
> **Example of a rule of your own:** A change to the reservation form is tried once with a full event
> before it is merged, because a full event must never accept a reservation.

## The AI note

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** Every pull request has the section *AI note: what I asked AI, and what I checked*,
> from the starter's pull request template. Keep that heading as it is. The note is one or two lines:
> what you asked an AI tool, and how you checked its answer. "No AI used" is a valid note. A pull
> request made with GitHub's **Revert** button needs no note. Write here what your team's notes say,
> and how you check what an AI tool wrote before you use it.
>
> **Example of a note:** *What I asked AI:* how to show "Full" on an event with no seats left. *What I
> checked:* on the preview, I reserved the last seat with made-up details. The event then showed
> "Full", and the form refused a second reservation.

## When someone stops contributing

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** Agree this now, while everyone is calm. Three or four lines are enough. Write:
> - what counts as stopping for your team, for example no answer for a number of days, or a task left
>   open after its date;
> - what you do first inside the pair, and then as a whole team, with a new task and a date;
> - how you record what happened: you change this agreement when you share the roles or the work again.
>
> **Example:** If a member does not answer for three working days, their pair partner contacts them
> directly. If a task is still open a week after its date, we agree a new date at the weekly meeting
> and, if needed, a smaller task. If it happens a second time, we share that member's tasks out again
> and update this agreement.
