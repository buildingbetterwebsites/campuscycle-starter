# Project brief

The short description of your site: who it is for and what it does. Its reader has never seen your
site, so write what a stranger needs to understand it. Fill in every place marked TODO. The boxes
marked Tip are guidance: the course's checks do not read them, and you may delete them when you are
done. Keep the brief to one page; the details belong in `docs/requirements.md`.

In a team, write under the title who wrote this document, by first name or GitHub username.

## What the site is for

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** Write two things.
> - The promise the site makes. Write it as one sentence: "[Your service] helps [who] to [do what],
>   so that [what the organisation gets]." The sentence names no feature and no product.
> - The organisation or topic the site is about: who runs it, what it offers, and whether it is real
>   or made up. Choose a real organisation only if it knows about the project and agrees.
>
> **Example:** Millbrook Library's events help people in the city to reserve a seat at a free event,
> so that the library knows before each event how many people are coming. Millbrook Library is a
> made-up city library. It runs author talks, a reading group and story hours for children.

## The two tasks, ranked

> [!TIP]
> **Guidance.** Write the primary task first: the task that must work before anything else. Write each
> task as an action and its outcome, with its type. The two tasks have different types, and at least
> one is transactional: the user sends something that the site saves. For each task, say what users
> lose when it fails. Then say in two or three sentences why the primary task must work first.
>
> **Example:** 1. Reserve a seat at an event (transactional). Outcome: the reservation is saved for an
> event that still had a seat, and the user sees an honest confirmation. When it fails, the library
> does not know how many people are coming. 2. Find the events of this month that suit you
> (informational). Outcome: the user knows the date, the place and the age group of each event. When it
> fails, a user can still ask at the library's desk. Reserving must work first, because nothing else
> tells the library how many people are coming.
